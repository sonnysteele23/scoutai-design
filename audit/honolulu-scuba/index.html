The five findings that matter

01

The hero background video is loaded over plain HTTP: `src="http://honoluluscubacompany.m3sites.com/.../background-vid.mp4"` on an HTTPS page.

Lighthouse best-practices 54 with 'is-on-https' failing. Mobile performance is 54 with LCP 16.4 s and TBT 125 ms - the heaviest-feeling site in this batch on a phone (desktop 76 / 2.3 s).

02

Search snippet is truncated at both ends: title is 76 characters ('Explore Oahu's Premier Scuba Diving Tours & Courses | Honolulu Scuba Company') and the description is 247 characters; Google cuts titles at ~60 and descriptions at ~155.

03

The schema knows the brand but not the shop: JSON-LD is Organization/WebSite/BreadcrumbList with no address, phone or hours, although the footer prints '670 Auahi St.

A1 Honolulu, HI 96813' and 'Monday - Saturday, 7 a.m. - 7 p.m.'. 'Scuba shop near me' answers depend on exactly those fields.

04

6 of 10 images have no alt - including the TripAdvisor and Yelp badges, and the Yelp badge is not even a link (no yelp.com href on the page).

Trust badges that cannot be clicked or read are decoration.

05

No Meta pixel / ads tag (two GA4 IDs suggest a tracking mix-up instead), and `user-scalable=0` in the viewport blocks pinch-zoom - an accessibility fail on a site where customers read dive schedules on phones.

These are measurements, not design opinions. Each one is something your current provider could have run in ten minutes.

What I would do in the first week

  • Change the video src to https:// (the m3sites host already serves HTTPS).
  • Cut the title to ~58 chars and the description to ~155.
  • Extend the JSON-LD to LocalBusiness/SportsActivityLocation with address, phone, hours, sameAs.
  • Alt text on all 10 images; link the Yelp badge to the Yelp page; remove user-scalable=0.
  • Consolidate to one GA4 ID; add a Meta pixel and fire a FareHarbor booking event.
