The five findings that matter

01

Half the sitemap is a wall for Google: 338 of 677 URLs are /articles/aad_education_library/...

and every one returns HTTP 403 (verified with a browser UA and a Googlebot UA). Google is invited to crawl 338 pages it is forbidden to read - crawl budget wasted and a 'Submitted URL blocked' error pile in Search Console.

02

The platform is a decade old: jQuery 1.11.1 (2014) is loaded; the page body is the Officite 'new-orleans' template; the server rejects HEAD requests with 501 (breaks some link checkers and uptime monitors).

Lighthouse best-practices will flag the vulnerable library.

03

Analytics is double-/triple-tagged: two GA4 properties (G-51L19HQQ46, G-EQH496LMM2) plus a dead Universal Analytics ID (UA-238591326-2, shut off by Google in 2024).

The practice is likely looking at the wrong property or inflated numbers.

04

No online booking.

'Make an appointment' leads to a form (appointment-request page) - no NexHealth/Zocdoc-style widget. Cosmetic patients book cash-pay procedures at 10 pm; a form means a callback the next business day.

05

No favicon declared (`favicon: []`), no Twitter card, no map embed or Google Maps link on the home page despite two locations; the hidden `<h2>Error!</h2>` modal and 'Javascript must be enabled' banner are template debris.

These are measurements, not design opinions. Each one is something your current provider could have run in ten minutes.

What I would do in the first week

  • Remove the /articles/ library from the sitemap or unblock it (ask Officite which) - today it is both listed and forbidden.
  • Delete the UA tag and pick one GA4 property.
  • Add <link rel=icon>, Twitter card tags and an embedded map per location.
  • Add an online booking widget (even a Calendly-style request) on the home page hero.
  • Ask Officite to update jQuery; if they cannot, that is the rebuild trigger.

Recommended: rebuild on a platform you own

A rebuild (WordPress with the existing MedicalClinic schema, per-provider pages, per-condition pages that the practice owns, online booking) removes the 403'd library, the 2014 JavaScript and the vendor lock. The six-provider, two-location practice is the strongest rebuild candidate in this batch.
